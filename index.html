Hocbigg - Architecture

Path to a free self-taught education in Architecture.

Last updated on: 2026-10-12

Introduction

Architecture is the synthesis of spatial design, cultural history, and physical engineering. It is the discipline concerned with how people organize, construct, and experience three-dimensional space, from a single room to a public institution. Studying architecture on your own builds visual literacy and spatial reasoning. It helps you see why buildings look the way they do, how they stand up, and how their form affects human behavior.

This curriculum is for absolute beginners with no background in design, drafting, or structural engineering. It needs no specialized software, studio access, or university enrollment. High school geometry and basic physical intuition are enough. The technical resources favor conceptual clarity, visual diagrams, and physical behavior over dense mathematical derivation.

The curriculum is organized into six foundational subjects arranged in two complementary phases:

  • Visual and conceptual foundations: Start with Architectural Design Fundamentals and Architectural Representation and Drawing, ideally together. The first establishes the discipline's spatial vocabulary of form, proportion, axes, datum, and hierarchy. The second supplies the drawing conventions (plans, sections, elevations, and axonometric projections) you need to express and analyze those ideas on paper.
  • Precedent and context: Move to Architectural History and Survey once you have a working grasp of architectural drawing. Studying historical precedents through drawings lets you analyze buildings as structural and spatial solutions to cultural, political, and material constraints, instead of memorizing stylistic periods.
  • Physical and technical realization: Take the technical subjects in order. Start with Structures to see how loads, tension, compression, and moments resolve through building forms. Next, Building Construction and Materials covers how those forces are realized in concrete, steel, wood, and masonry assemblies. Finish with Environmental Systems, which covers how passive orientation, daylighting, heat transfer, and acoustics determine interior comfort and energy performance.

This page covers only the foundational core: the conceptual, graphic, and technical knowledge expected of any undergraduate architecture student. It does not cover specialized professional tracks, CAD/BIM software tutorials, or legal licensing requirements.

Once you have this grounding, you can branch out depending on your goals:

  • Build physical models, technical drawing sets, and analytical drawings with the step-by-step guides in Projects.
  • Explore specialized domains, such as computational design, facade engineering, mass timber, or urban housing, in Advanced Topics.

Curriculum

Architectural Design Fundamentals

The principles of spatial organization, form, proportion, scale, and ordering systems that architects use to conceptualize the built environment.

The Architectural Imagination (Harvard University via edX) - A video-based introduction to design thinking and spatial ideas through historical analyses of exemplary buildings and guided drawing exercises. It is the interactive entry point to the visual texts below.

Architecture: Form, Space, and Order (Francis D. K. Ching) - The standard visual reference for architectural vocabulary. Study it alongside the Harvard course to see how primary elements, planar organizations, and proportioning systems work diagrammatically.

Analysing Architecture (Simon Unwin) - A complementary analytical guide that builds on Ching's formal vocabulary by showing how to deconstruct buildings and extract spatial strategies from real architecture.

Architectural Representation and Drawing

The conventions and techniques of architectural visualization: orthographic projections, paraline drawings, perspective views, and freehand sketching.

Architectural Graphics (Francis D. K. Ching) - The foundational manual on technical drafting conventions, lineweight hierarchy, orthographic projection (plans, sections, elevations), axonometric views, and architectural lettering.

30X40 Design Workshop (YouTube) - A video studio companion to Ching's manual that demonstrates physical drawing workflows, line-drawing techniques, and freehand sketching on paper.

Architectural History and Survey

A global survey of architectural development across cultures, eras, and technologies, tracing how social, political, and material factors have shaped the built environment over time.

A Global History of Architecture (Francis D. K. Ching, Mark M. Jarzombek, and Vikramaditya Prakash) - The primary comprehensive survey text. It organizes global architectural developments chronologically instead of focusing only on the Western canon.

Introduction to the History and Theory of Architecture (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A study guide built around the Ching/Jarzombek text above, with lecture outlines, reading assignments, and comparative analytical essay prompts.

The Ten Books on Architecture (Vitruvius, translated by Morris Hicky Morgan) (Project Gutenberg) - A foundational primary source from classical antiquity. Read it selectively alongside the survey materials to examine early treatises on proportion, utility, and building methods.

Structures

The physical and mechanical principles governing how forces and loads move through structural elements to reach equilibrium and stability in buildings.

Basic Concepts of Structural Design for Architecture Students (Anahita Khodadadi, Portland State University via Open Textbook Library) - An accessible open textbook on structural loads, force vectors, equilibrium, and structural configurations, with minimal mathematical complexity.

Why Buildings Stand Up: The Strength of Architecture (Mario Salvadori) - A narrative, conceptual companion to Khodadadi's textbook. It explains how tension, compression, shear, beams, arches, and trusses work in famous historical and modern structures.

Building Construction and Materials

The assemblies, detailing methods, and material systems through which architectural designs are built and weatherproofed.

Building Construction Illustrated (Francis D. K. Ching) - The core visual manual on building assemblies, foundation systems, envelope enclosures, and structural joinery, taught through annotated technical drawings.

Fundamentals of Building Construction: Materials and Methods (Edward Allen and Joseph Iano) - The comprehensive standard textbook. It supplies the technical narrative, material properties, and job-site assembly methods that correspond to Ching's graphic details.

Building Technology I: Materials and Construction (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A university-level syllabus with lecture notes, structural morphology analyses, and quizzes to test your understanding of wood, steel, concrete, and masonry construction.

Environmental Systems

The principles and technologies governing indoor comfort, solar orientation, passive heating and cooling, daylighting, and acoustics in architecture.

Heating, Cooling, Lighting: Sustainable Design Strategies Towards Net Zero Architecture (Norbert Lechner and Patricia Andrasik) - The definitive design textbook on climate-responsive architecture, focused on schematic-stage passive solar heating, natural ventilation, sun shading, and daylighting strategies.

Environmental Technologies in Buildings (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A rigorous course that builds on Lechner's passive strategies with lecture slides, physical performance calculations, and computational daylighting and energy simulations.
